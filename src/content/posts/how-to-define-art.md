This question has been in my mind for a long time, given that I was a philosophy bachelor student before, we definitely had lots of discussion of this already. I remember my previous answer was that I said I think everything is art, that it's more an objective matter of its own, meaning whoever considers something to be art, makes it art. (Regarding all those ridiculous things in contemporary museums, such as toilets or banana peels...)

But the reading materials had changed my mind, like enlightening fireworks in my head.

## Art as a Biological Act

We had been thinking within the Western frame of art, suggesting that things performed, showcased, or exhibited in museums define what art is. However, according to Ellen Dissanayake (2008), art is a **biological** thing. It exists in a sense of "control" over the uncertainty of life situations. Art is an action, and through making art, or in her definition, "making special", the act itself is an evolutionary adaptation for stress reduction.

She gives an example from the Trobriand Islanders, where their ceremonial seafaring canoes (*masawa*), used for long-distance Kula trade, are carefully carved, while ordinary fishing boats are only roughly decorated, and personal everyday canoes have no carvings at all. The more important and uncertain the journey, the more "special" the object becomes. It's not about beauty or showing off, it's a way to mark significance, and to make the ritual "work".

There are also studies on the chemicals produced when people sing or dance together. Tarr (2014) calls this "behavioral synchrony", and it triggers endorphins that promote individual well-being and social bonding through music. That explains why we see musical gatherings in all kinds of ancient and traditional cultures, like the work songs of West African communities or the harvest festivals of Taiwanese Indigenous tribes such as the Amis people.

This is an aspect I had never considered, and it feels so important to recognize as the foundation of the entire Art and Well-being framework.

## Why We Create Stuff

I think this explains why I started making music, because it makes me feel better. It also explains some miserable people are almost addicted to creating, and why some workaholics can't stop creating any kind of art. Because of the sense of having "control" in something.

It also reminds me of *Rewire* by Nicole Vignola (2024), where she discusses labeling emotions as a way to decrease anxiety or fear. When we feel anxiety or fear, the amygdala in the limbic system is highly activated. But by labeling those emotions, we can activate the right ventrolateral prefrontal cortex, engaging the logical part of the brain to gain more control over these emotions.

In summary, I think art is an act of labeling human anxiety, transferring uncertainty into an actual symbol.

## What Is Well-being Anyway?

We also discussed the definition of "health" and "well-being." It's kind of tricky for me, because I don't think there's a specific word to translate "well-being" in my native language. But I understand it as a person fully developing a complete body-mind-soul, where these three vectors point in the same direction for life.

Sometimes the brain fights against the mind and somehow fails to nurture our soul. It's certainly a lifelong challenge, we have to take care of our physical body, learn the mechanism of our psychological states, and explore the divinity within. All of these matter.

Art, as Richard Hickman (2016) suggested, is an essential biological behavior for psychological health, because creating and conferring aesthetic significance is an instinctive desire of human beings.

## A Curse in How We Teach Art

With this perspective, I would say that some ways of teaching art, including elite education systems, competitive environments, are really a "curse" on this biological expression. The high pressure suppresses a human being's natural impulse to create. It's sad that people have to re-learn how to freely create art later in life.

But I believe we can still find a way to fix this. To discover the true essence of all kinds of art and liberate the true potential of it and the connection with human beings.
